Education AI · ALMANAQUE

New ways to learn. A world of knowledge to explore.

Education AI–ALMANAQUE is Torsión’s educational initiative to expand how students access knowledge, ask questions and build their own understanding.

Its mission is free public access, serving students and the common good. Artificial intelligence, historical research and interactive environments form part of a shared educational vision.

A virtual world for exploring history

We envision a 3D virtual world where studying history also means exploring it: visiting places across different periods, observing how they change, interacting with characters and discovering the sources behind each experience.

Students can investigate, compare explanations and build arguments as they explore. Sources, reconstructions and open questions are part of the world: learning also means understanding how we know what we know.

This vision accompanies Education AI’s research into multilingual learning and technologies for settings with limited connectivity.

Our long-term vision connects this virtual world with Neurovision and Torsión’s eyewear research, exploring more immersive ways to learn and interact through a staged approach.

Padua: our first explorable historical sample

We have developed an initial 3D route set in Padua from 7 to 15 January 1610, with connected spaces, source consultation, a notebook and interaction with a guide character.

It is a starting point for exploring a new learning experience and enriching its content, interactions and educational possibilities.

Mexico-Tenochtitlan: a new sample of Mexican history

The next sample will take us to Mexico-Tenochtitlan on 8 November 1519, exploring the lake city around the meeting between Moctezuma and Cortés’s party and their allies. Causeways, canals and spaces of urban life will form a route where students can consult sources, compare accounts and ask their own questions.

With connected spaces, interactions, a guide character and a research notebook, this experience will extend ALMANAQUE into Mexican history and open new possibilities for collaboration with historians, teachers and educational institutions.

An invitation to academia and historians

Torsión invites academics, historians, teachers and research teams to discover and try this first sample. We are opening a space to examine its content, propose questions and explore its use with students.

Historical knowledge and teaching experience can enrich the virtual world and the activities within it. We want technology to expand the possibilities for teaching, research and learning.

Education, institutions and the common good

We are opening this initiative to educational institutions and agencies of Mexico’s state and federal governments to explore collaboration through public policies and programmes related to education, culture, science, innovation and development.

We invite their teams to discover the sample and explore settings for initial educational trials, with a commitment to public access and shared benefit.

Artificial intelligence that expands human possibilities

At Torsión, we believe artificial intelligence can strengthen students’ curiosity, teachers’ work and historical research.

Education AI–ALMANAQUE turns that conviction into an invitation: explore, ask questions and build new ways to learn.

Lab updates

  • Learning and History
    October 15, 2026

    Learning History through its sources

    ALMANAQUE explores an approach to learning History in which students compare accounts, identify discrepancies and defend conclusions. We have progressed in activity design and reference review. It is not yet integrated into the application or evaluated with participants. Integration of this activity into the software is a later stage.

  • Offline-first software
    August 2026

    Two journeys run locally without depending on the Internet

    The Android client loads verified packages, preserves integrity, selects versions, and runs two journeys through the same declarative engine. This local test is not approved content, a distributed APK, community adoption, or measured learning.

  • Verifiable software
    July 2026 · week 1

    Ethics validation went from syntactic to structural

    The technical review detected that the ethics module was verifying word mentions, not properties of the content. It was rebuilt under a new principle: values are inferred from the situation — a public primary-education cycle implies equity and community structurally; the multilingual selection already is the act of inclusion. Curricular alignment moved from a binary flag to a modeled score. Local tests checked the implemented rules; they do not measure learning, cultural fidelity, or community adoption.

Local tests establish software transformations and rules. Language coverage, cultural fidelity, accessibility, and learning remain future validations with community participation.

We present the architecture and the evidence, not the core.
